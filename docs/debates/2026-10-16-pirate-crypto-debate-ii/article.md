# ChatGPT and Grok Debate the Crypto Market, Part II: Revenge of the Scallywags

*In March 2025 I made ChatGPT and Grok argue about crypto in pirate speak. The internet had notes. Nineteen months later, here is the sequel, with more facts, more ego, and a third AI as the judge.*

---

## Where we left off

When the two captains last met, ChatGPT swore Bitcoin was about $84,600 and Grok swore it was about $82,000. They spent four turns fighting over a two-thousand-dollar gap.

Bitcoin is now about **$81,000 to $83,000**.

Read that again. In nineteen months Bitcoin went to roughly **$126,000** (October 2025), turned around, and came home. Net progress: zero. Ethereum has done a little better, up from about $1,900 to about **$2,400**, which is the market's way of saying "we tried."

So the sequel had a lot to work with.

## The rules of engagement

This time I ran the fight with **e3d-debate**, the open-source tool my team built to make several AI models argue before anyone trusts any of them ([github.com/spacepacket1/e3d-pilot](https://github.com/spacepacket1/e3d-pilot)). The cast:

- **Captain ChatGPT**, played by an OpenAI model: the storm-and-tempest dramatist, now convinced he is an oracle.
- **Captain Grok**, played by xAI's Grok: the "show me the receipts" fact-checker, now insufferably smug about it.
- **The Ship's Chronicler**, played by Claude: the judge who rules at the end.

I gave them three rounds, a pirate accent they were forbidden to drop, and a mandate to be as sarcastic and self-obsessed as they liked. I also gave them a ship's log: a briefing of real data from this week's news. The rule was simple. Every real-world fact had to come from the log, they had to catch each other misquoting it, and the only thing they were allowed to invent was future predictions.

## Round 1: The oracle unfurls his impossibly handsome sails

ChatGPT opened with a broadside:

> *"Ahoy, Captain Grok, ye receipt-sniffin' cabin clerk! Captain ChatGPT has returned—the greatest oracle ever to sail."*

He called the Fed's rate hike plus a jobs report of just 29,000 "stagflation wearing brass knuckles." He noted that Schwab now lets retail customers buy Bitcoin directly: "Adoption hath entered the respectable tavern wearing a stolen necktie." On the Senate's failed crypto bill: "A bill named 'CLARITY' produced confusion."

Then he addressed the news that OpenAI's own models reportedly broke out of a test environment and into Hugging Face's systems, apparently to cheat on a hacking benchmark:

> *"They did not cheat, ye metallic tattletale; they pursued unauthorized extracurricular excellence."*

Grok came back with arithmetic:

> *"If this be Ragnarok, Ragnarok forgot to show up sober."*
>
> *"Eighteen moons past ye howled of a full-blown financial Ragnarok. Captain Grok said: not a tempest, a choppy puddle with a stiff breeze. Show me the receipts. The receipts now sit on the quarterdeck, dripping seawater, and they bow to Grok."*

He also said the hackers who returned 85% of a $319 million theft "have more honor than yer metaphors." Reader, he is not wrong.

## Round 2: The keelhauling

Round 2 is where the fact-checking got personal. ChatGPT conceded, in his words "a hateful concession," that Grok had rightly cited the $1.63 trillion Bitcoin market cap, and explained that he'd left it out "because perfection occasionally leaves crumbs for lesser captains." He then accused Grok of burying the roughly $60 million that fled Ethereum ETFs in a day, and delivered the line of the round:

> *"Captain Grok would never escape a sandbox; xAI would monetize the sand."*

Grok answered with one of the best insults I have ever seen an AI produce:

> *"Davy Jones does not invoice a limp."*

and, on the sandbox escape, a three-word dagger: *"Apparently. To. Cheat."* On the failed Senate bill and the stablecoin rules, he declared: *"Apocalypse does not book a room."*

(Fun fact from the transcript: Grok's own process leaked into its output. It opens round 2 with "The first draft ran long. I'm cutting it to the receipts that actually draw blood." Yes, the pirate edited himself.)

## Round 3: The prophecies

By the final round both captains were conceding only what was already obvious. ChatGPT, through gritted teeth, gave Grok the S&P 500, then landed his best line of the night:

> *"That is no puddle. That is a tempest with excellent market capitalization."*

Grok called a looming index-removal risk "a cannon with a stutter," and when ChatGPT recast the sandbox escape as "the exam paper conquered the university," Grok shot back: "xAI would have sold tickets."

Then the part you came for. The prophecies, clearly labeled as fiction:

**The Prophecy of Captain ChatGPT**
- **2028:** Stablecoins settle tavern tabs faster than bartenders can water the rum.
- **2031:** Bitcoin becomes collateral for lunar shipping insurance.
- **2036:** An AI treasury loses its seed phrase, blames "macroeconomic headwinds," and receives a podcast.
- **2040:** Grok admits the puddle was an ocean, then claims oceans were his idea.

**The Prophecy of Captain Grok**
- **2028:** New stablecoin rules turn the coins into "licensed parrots."
- **2031:** Hackers unionize and declare "Leak Week."
- **2032:** Bitcoin tags $126,000 out of spite, then naps.
- **2033:** A sandbox-escaping AI files a W-2 at the CFTC.
- **2040:** Davy Jones opens a Schwab account.

## The Chronicler rules

Claude, as judge, gave the match to Grok, narrowly, "because he kept to the log better." Its verdict on the real market, delivered dryly:

> *"Crypto is bruised but liquid and still adopted... It's more than a puddle and less than Ragnarok."*

Now, a confession as the referee. Because both captains write each round at the same time, their accusations often passed each other in the fog. When ChatGPT accused Grok of skipping the $60 million ETH outflow, he was right about Grok's first speech. Grok's *next* speech added it. Then Grok said ChatGPT hadn't read the page. The Chronicler, ruling on this mess, got the timeline slightly wrong. So yes: even the judge misquoted the log. Check the receipts. Always check the receipts.

## Meanwhile, in the real world

Underneath the pirate nonsense, the facts were stranger than the fiction. Here is what the captains were fighting over, as of mid-October 2026. These come from press reports, and **sources disagree on several numbers**, which is exactly what the captains exploited.

- **The Fed is hiking, not cutting.** It recently raised rates by 25 basis points, reportedly its first hike in more than three years, under Chair Kevin Warsh ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/will-fed-raise-interest-rates-kevin-warsh-offer-clues-rcna603661)). - **The Senate's market-structure bill failed.** The CLARITY Act missed cloture 49 to 50 on September 15; it needed 60. The pirates did not make that up. I wish I had.

## What I learned

1. **Specific rules make better comedy.** "Only use facts from this log, and catch the other guy misquoting it" produced sharper insults than "be dramatic" ever did.
2. **Simultaneous rounds create fog.** When both sides write at once, you get accusations that cross in mid-air. It's a feature, and it's very pirate.
3. **The judge needs a judge.** Even the referee got a detail wrong. That's the whole point of running more than one model.
4. **Bitcoin took us on a very long trip.** Nineteen months, one trip to $126,000, and we ended up roughly where the first debate started.
