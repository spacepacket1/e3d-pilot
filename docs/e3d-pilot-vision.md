*E3D Pilot: From Developer Questions to Developer Talent Intelligence—and an Expert Marketplace for Everyone*

We started E3D Pilot by asking a simple question: *What happens when developers stop relying on one AI and let independent models challenge each other?* Now the commercial destination is becoming sharper: not only helping developers answer coding and architecture questions, but eventually helping them *demonstrate their expertise* and helping hiring teams examine job-relevant technical evidence. Think a developer recruiting assistant grounded in real work—not an AI that decides whom to hire.

The infrastructure needed to do that unlocks something much bigger.

*The problem:* Social media is full of brilliant prompts and skill files people are told to copy, paste, maintain and somehow combine. That is a fragile, manual workflow that doesn't scale, and popularity tells us little about quality.

*The vision:* E3D Pilot becomes a cross-assistant network of *reusable AI experts* and *dynamically assembled councils*.

• *Create:* Tell your AI, “Build me a skeptical Ethereum security expert,” or “Make this approach a reusable expert.” Pilot generates a private, testable, versioned expert, with explicit tools, evidence requirements and limits. Nothing becomes public without the creator's approval.
• *Discover and publish:* Experts and council recipes can be shared, searched, liked/disliked, saved, forked and improved. The existing Debate Gallery provides public examples, attribution and lineage.
• *Auto Council:* Describe your question. A lead AI selects appropriate specialists, model diversity and permitted tools, explains why, and assembles the council. People can override it or choose a proven team.
• *Measure:* Likes help discovery, but *verified findings, reliability, unique contributions, cost and real outcomes* determine whether an expert or team is effective. We should measure lift over a strong single model, not manufacture consensus.
• *Use anywhere:* MCP-first. ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and other clients are where people work. Our website is the canonical discovery, identity, provenance and publishing surface—not a forced replacement chat app.

*Where developer recruiting fits:* A candidate could authorize a review of a résumé, GitHub projects and other work samples against a job description. Pilot would produce a traceable evidence map, uncertainties and technical interview questions for people to evaluate. This is a potential high-value wedge, but *not* automated candidate scoring, ranking or hiring. We must validate technical quality, consent, bias/privacy and employment-law obligations before any employer-facing launch.

*We asked the actual Pilot Council first.* Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok debated this in two rounds. *Majority verdict: SHIP the expert/council foundation.* They did not unanimously endorse the rollout: GPT favored earlier public discovery, while Claude and Gemini argued for measurement before marketplace scale; recruiting should be a later gated pilot. The strongest message was: *prove useful intelligence first; don't mistake likes for expertise.*

*Proposed sequence:* (0) audit/reconcile our existing specs and Gallery PRs; (1) private/versioned experts and preset councils; (2) explainable Auto Council over MCP; (3) verified outcome measurement; (4) curated public expert/recipe sharing and reactions; (5) candidate-controlled developer talent evidence pilot; (6) optional paid expert offerings only after demand.

This extends—not replaces—our work on Debate Gallery, public contributions, challenges, model dissent and MCP distribution. We've already built much of the identity, reaction, provenance and billing infrastructure. The opportunity is to turn that into a growing network of *usable, attributable, measurable intelligence*.

*The product promise:* *Don't copy a prompt. Find—or create—the right expert. Let the right council do the work. See the evidence.*
